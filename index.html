Maya

How It All Started

I had this idea.. build a drone that could both fly and move on the ground. But before I could even think about making it multimodal, I needed to get my hands dirty with flying a drone first....and well that was a task in itself.

Cool, I had one lying around. Except... it didn't work. Classic. So I went looking for a new one, and guess what? Every single flight controller was out of stock. Everywhere.

Then I had this random thought: Why can't I just make my own?

And here's the kicker...this brilliant idea hit me the night before I had an exam. You know how motivation hits different when you're procrastinating? Yeah, that's when I decided to start designing my own flight controller.

Mind you, I had absolutely zero prior knowledge of PCB designing.

One and a Half Months of Chaos

What followed was a month and a half of me diving headfirst into PCB design, learning about microcontrollers, sensors and all the tiny components that make flight controllers tick. There were late nights, failed designs, and moments where I questioned my life choices.

But you know what? I did it. I designed my own flight controller from scratch.

The Design Process

And that's how Maya_V was born!

Plot Twist: It Had Faults 🙃

After getting Maya_V made, I discovered faults in the design. Not ideal. But honestly? Kind of expected for a first PCB made from zero knowledge in six weeks.

Going through the board carefully, it became clear that debugging this version was going to be a nightmare - the layout made it really hard to trace what was going wrong where. So instead of endlessly poking at v1, I made the call to go back to the drawing board and start fresh with a second version, this time designed with debuggability as a first-class priority.

Maya V2 - Designing Smarter

Work in progress

The goal with V2 isn't just "fix the bugs." It's to rethink the layout and architecture so that when something goes wrong (and it will), I can actually figure out why. That means cleaner routing, better test points, more deliberate component placement - all the stuff you learn the hard way.

V1

Designed from scratch with zero PCB experience. Got made. Had faults. Hard to debug.

Right now

Re-planning V2 with a focus on making it easier to trace, test, and fix. Constantly iterating on the design.

Soon™

Get V2 made, actually fly it, and finally get to the multimodal stuff.

Meanwhile - Learning to Fly on ZeroDrag's FC

Along with designing V2... I got hold of ZeroDrag's flight controller and I've been using it to actually practice flying - flashing Betaflight, tuning, and getting reps in.

It has been... a rough ride. Betaflight has a learning curve that doesn't really announce itself. But that's kind of the point - the more I struggle with it now, the more I'll understand about what my own FC needs to handle.
